"Please" across Languages

I beg

  • Polish: proszę/prosimy
    • Seems to have only a slight preference to exist clause-finally. Also fairly common clause-initially.
    • Inflects for number of speaker (singular/plural).
    • Takes an infinitive.

if it pleases you

  • English: please
    • Three valid positions: clause-initial, clause-final, or immediate preverbal. In simple clauses, the first feels by far to be the most common and neutral.
    • Syntactically, this is an adverb and therefore frequently comes between the verb and its auxiliary (or 'to', if infinitive): eg. "Tell him to please stop calling me." and "If you would please turn the computer on, we can begin."
    • Also frequently construed as an interrogative with the modal can/could or will/would: eg. "Would you please not shoot the pigeons?"
    • Clause is otherwise inflected in the imperative mood.
  • French: s'il vous/te plaît
    • Seems to have a strong preference to lie at the end of the clause.
    • Inflects to listener, in politeness/number (these categories are somewhat collapsed in French).
    • Clause is inflected in the imperative mood.
